abstract: "Nonverbal signals are thought to carry political meaning beyond words, yet whom politicians seek eye contact with remains largely unknown. I study directed eye contact as a continuous behavioural signal, grounded in social identity theory and costly signalling, using all 15,553 video-recorded speeches from the 19th German Bundestag (2017-2021). Confrontational eye contact tracks political status: politicians who can most afford to send it do so most, correlating with more public attention, while those who cannot afford it avoid it altogether, correlating with less. Female and older MPs more often look straight ahead, avoiding eye contact with any party, while male MPs do the reverse, most consistently with the Far Right. Seniority cuts both ways: longer-tenured MPs seek it more, while older MPs seek it less. Beyond the chamber, gaze at the Far Right correlates with elevated public attention, while its complete avoidance correlates with an even larger drop."
